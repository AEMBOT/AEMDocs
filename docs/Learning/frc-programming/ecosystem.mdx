# Intro to the FRC Ecosystem
As an FRC programmer, there's a relatively small number of potential hardware and software targets your team will probably end up using.
This page will walk you through the ones you'll be interacting with as an AEMBOT programmer.

## Hardware
### The RoboRIO / SystemCore
<img src={RoboRIO} height='300px' alt="The RoboRIO from National Instruments. Labelled are features such as the USB ports, an SD card slot, the ethernet port, and the reset and user buttons." /> <img src={SystemCore} height='300px' alt="The new SystemCore device from Limelight." />
The RoboRIO (pre-2027) or SystemCore (2027 and beyond) are the primary control devices for the robot. They both run linux-based operating
systems, and can have Java; C++; Python; LabVIEW; or, in the case of SystemCore, Blockly code deployed to them. We'll also usually plug a
USB thumb stick into it to contain logs.

Generally speaking, your main method of connecting to the device will be via the radio (more on that below). If you're connected this way, you'll
be able to access your RIO/SystemCore at the IP address 10.64.43.2. You can SSH into the RIO via the following command:

</Tabs>

Before each season (or sometimes during the season), you'll need to image your RoboRIO or SystemCore with the latest operating system. You can find
instructions for the RIO [here](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/2025/docs/zero-to-robot/step-3/roborio2-imaging.html), and instructions for SystemCore
[here](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/latest/docs/zero-to-robot/step-3/imaging-your-systemcore.html). 

### Radio
<img src={VHRadio} width="200px" alt="A VividHosting 109 radio." />
The robot's radio will be your primary method of communication with it. As of 2026, we use the VH-109 radio. There are four main ways to connect to this 
radio, depending on how it's configured.
- **Via a direct ethernet connection to it.** This will be your only method of connection while at comps in the pit.
- **Via the FMS (Field Management System).** This is the system FIRST uses to manage teams' robots and connect them to their driver stations. At a comp,
  you'll need to take your radio up to be configured for FMS by event staff.
- **Via Wi-Fi (setting the radio up as an access point).** Your radio will essentially act as a wi-fi router. You can connect to your robot from your
  device's wi-fi interface.
- **Via another radio.** This is the method that we use most often in the shop. You'll connect your laptop via ethernet to a stationary radio, and it
  will communicate wirelessly with the robot's radio. The below diagram illustrates this setup.

<img src="https://docs.wpilib.org/en/latest/_images/VH-109-2-radios.drawio.svg" />

See [here](https://docs.wpilib.org/en/latest/docs/zero-to-robot/step-3/radio-programming.html) for a guide to configuring your radio.

### CTRE Hardware
Cross the Road Electronics (CTRE) is probably our most used vendor for electronics on our robots; they produce almost all of our sensors and motors.
Sometimes you may hear them called Phoenix devices, since we use the [Phoenix 6 libary](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/api-reference/api-usage/index.html) to control them.

The majority of CTRE's devices (all of the ones we use) operate on a CAN bus. As opposed to directly individually wiring devices to the RIO/SystemCore, all 
devices are interconnected by a bus. Each device has a numerical CAN ID, and every message contains the ID that the message is originating from or targetting.
The RoboRIO has one integrated CAN bus using CAN 2.0, and the SystemCore has five integrated CAN-FD (faster) busses. CTRE also offers the CANivore to create a 
seperate CAN-FD bus, but this'll be disallowed in 2028. In older RIO-based bots, you'll often see a seperate CANivore bus for the drivetrain.

The CTRE devices we use most are:
- **TalonFX**: This is the **motor controller** used for CTRE's Kraken and Falcon motors, and probably the CTRE device we interact with most.
  - [CTRE Hardware Reference](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/hardware-reference/talonfx/index.html)
  - [CTRE Software Docs](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/api-reference/device-specific/talonfx/index.html)
  - /* TODO add AEMLib docs */
- **CANcoder**: The CANcoder is, as the name indicates, an **absolute encoder** that can communicate via CAN. It senses the magnetic field of a magnet attached to
  your rotating mechanism, thereby determining its rotation. Unlike a **relative encoder**, it does not lose its state when it loses power. It's especially good 
  for mechanisms that won't have a very precise starting config, and generally good for any mechanism where its position is important since it's resistant to
  unexpected power cuts or brown-outs.
  - [CTRE Hardware Reference](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/hardware-reference/cancoder/index.html)
  - /* TODO add AEMLib docs */
- **CANrange**: The CANrange is a **time-of-flight sensor**; it fires off a laser and measures how long it takes the light to bounce back to determine the distance
  to the surface it hits. If you're familiar with game dev or rendering, it's very similar to a raycast!
  - [CTRE Hardware Reference](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/hardware-reference/canrange/index.html)
  - /* TODO add AEMLib docs */
- **Pigeon 2**: The Pigeon 2 is an IMU; it provides us with the acceleration and rotation of the robot. We generally use it as our exclusive source of truth for
  robot yaw, since it's generally substantially more accurate than dead reckoning or vision.
  - [CTRE Hardware Reference](https://v6.docs.ctr-electronics.com/en/stable/docs/hardware-reference/pigeon2/index.html)

### Limelight
<img src={Limelight} width="400px" alt="The Limelight 4, a camera and coprocessor encased in a rectangular black box." />
We use Limelights for our computer vision (CV). Our most common use for them is to detect field-mounted AprilTags (tags similar to QR codes, which each have a 
unique numerical ID assigned to them), and use their positions relative to the camera to determine the robot's field position.
